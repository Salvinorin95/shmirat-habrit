# A Plan for Guarding the Covenant: Be Master of Yourself

> "Who is mighty? One who conquers his inclination." (Mishnah, Pirkei Avot 4:1)

This plan is for a man who wants to break free from porn and compulsive masturbation. It isn't built on guilt, and it isn't built on willpower alone. It's built on understanding yourself, on an environment that protects you, on routine, and on people beside you.
You don't need to be perfect to start. It's enough to start today.

## 1. Why

This struggle isn't meant to make you suffer. It's meant to help you live better. Many men who walk this path describe similar things:

- **Focus:** your mind is freer for work, creativity and study.
- **Energy:** fewer evenings that end in emptiness and time wasted in front of a screen.
- **Confidence:** when you keep the word you gave yourself, you start to trust yourself.
- **Presence in relationships:** you see people, and women in particular, as human beings rather than as stimulation. That prepares you for a real relationship.
- **Spiritual connection:** in the language of the Sages this is **shmirat habrit**, guarding the covenant: protecting your holiness and your bond with God.

> An honest note: these are personal experiences many people describe, not a medical promise. You won't find made-up "studies" or magic numbers here. What is clear: a habit you control is better than a habit that controls you.

## 2. Understanding the urge

The urge doesn't come from nowhere. Most of the time it's a response to something else: tiredness, loneliness, stress or boredom. Porn is a quick, false "solution" to a real need.

### HALT: four risky states
| State | What happens | What you really need |
|---|---|---|
| **H**ungry | Your body is depleted, patience is short | Eat something real |
| **A**ngry (or stressed) | You want to "release" it | Move, talk, write |
| **L**onely | Missing closeness and connection | A conversation with a real person |
| **T**ired | Self-control is low | **Go to sleep** |

### Other common triggers
- Boredom and unplanned time
- Phone in bed, especially late at night
- Scrolling social media ("just a peek")
- Being home alone for long stretches
- After a big success ("I deserve it") or after a failure ("everything's ruined anyway")

> "The evil inclination rules only over what the eyes see." (Talmud, Sotah 8a)
> The battle starts long before the hard moment. It starts with what you let in through your eyes.

## 3. Technical defense system

Willpower runs out at the end of a long day. A system doesn't. **Build an environment where falling is hard and the good choice is easy.**

> Joseph the righteous didn't debate with temptation: "He left his garment in her hand, and fled, and went outside." (Genesis 39:12). Sometimes strength simply means running away, and closing the door in advance.

### Defense checklist
- [ ] **The phone doesn't enter the bedroom.** Buy a simple alarm clock and charge the phone in the living room.
- [ ] **DNS filtering on the network or device**, for example Cloudflare for Families (`1.1.1.3` and `1.0.0.3`), which blocks adult content.
- [ ] **Phone filtering:** turn on the built-in content restrictions (Screen Time on iPhone, Digital Wellbeing / Family Link on Android) and block adult sites.
- [ ] **No private browsing (Incognito).** Block it where possible.
- [ ] **App time limits** for apps that lead to endless scrolling.
- [ ] **Someone else holds the restrictions password**, a friend or chavruta (study partner), not you.
- [ ] **Accountability software** that sends a report to your partner.
- [ ] **The computer in an open space**, not in bed, and no working on it late at night.
- [ ] **Clean-up:** delete files, bookmarks and accounts, and unfollow accounts that lead there.

> Filtering doesn't need to be perfect. It needs to create **friction**: a few seconds in which you remember who you want to be.

## 4. Daily routine

A full, intentional day leaves the inclination much less room. "I created the evil inclination, and I created Torah as its antidote." (Talmud, Kiddushin 30b).

### Morning
- [ ] Get up at a fixed time, no phone for the first 30 minutes
- [ ] Modeh Ani / prayer / a moment of intention for the day
- [ ] Movement: a workout, a walk, or at least a few minutes of stretching and push-ups
- [ ] Cold shower (optional): a minute of cold water at the end of your shower, a small daily practice in facing discomfort
- [ ] Write one line: "My goal today is..."

### During the day
- [ ] Work in focused blocks, with short breaks **away from** the screen
- [ ] Regular meals (the H in HALT)
- [ ] At least one conversation with a real person
- [ ] Study, even 10-15 minutes

### Evening
- [ ] A fixed "screens off" time (for example an hour before bed)
- [ ] The phone stays outside the bedroom
- [ ] A short daily review: what went well, what was hard
- [ ] **The bedtime Shema (Kriat Shema al hamita)**: close the day in holiness and quiet
- [ ] Enough sleep. Tiredness is one of the strongest triggers.

## 5. Emergency protocol: "It's hard right now"

The urge feels like it will last forever, but it comes in waves: it rises, peaks and falls. Your goal isn't "to defeat the inclination forever", but **to get through this wave**.

### Step by step
1. **Stop and name it.** Say out loud: "This is an urge. It will pass."
2. **Leave the room.** Now, no arguing. Change places, ideally go outside.
3. **20 push-ups** (or squats, or running in place). Get the energy out of your body.
4. **The 10-minute rule:** "I'm not deciding now. I'm waiting 10 minutes." Usually the wave subsides.
5. **Urge surfing:** slow breathing, 4 seconds in and 6 seconds out. Notice where in your body you feel the urge and just watch it, without acting.
6. **Call someone.** You don't have to say what it's about. You can text your partner: "It's hard for me right now."
7. **Check HALT:** am I hungry? angry? lonely? tired? Take care of the real need.
8. **Read your "why"** (section 1).
9. **A chapter or psalm of Tehillim / a short prayer in your own words.**

### Emergency card (screenshot or print it)
```
Urge = wave. It passes.
1. Leave the room
2. 20 push-ups
3. Wait 10 minutes
4. Text your partner
5. Eat / sleep / talk
```

## 6. Connection and community

Very often what feeds this habit is **loneliness**. Porn promises closeness and delivers emptiness. The real remedy is real connection.

- **Friends:** invest in a few deep friendships. A meal, a shared workout, a phone call.
- **Chavruta:** regular study with a friend builds both connection and commitment.
- **Accountability partner:** one person you trust, whom you report to every day or every week and can message in a hard moment. No judgment, in both directions.
- **Community:** synagogue, a training group, meetups in your professional field, volunteering.
- **Relationships:** if you want a meaningful relationship, act on it in a way that fits you and your values: a matchmaker (shidduchim), introductions through friends, community events. Working on guarding the covenant is part of preparing for a relationship. It teaches you to be present, faithful and respectful.

## 7. What to do after a fall

> "For a righteous man falls seven times and rises again." (Proverbs 24:16)

The verse doesn't say the righteous man doesn't fall. It says he **rises**. A fall is not the end of the road. It's information.

### What not to do
- ❌ Don't punish yourself, and don't slide into "everything's ruined anyway"
- ❌ Don't keep going "because I already fell". The binge is the real damage.
- ❌ Don't hide from your partner

### What to do: the 24-hour reset
1. **Stop now.** Close the screen and leave the room.
2. **Shower, food, sleep.** Bring your body back into balance.
3. **Tell your partner**, briefly and honestly.
4. **Analyze without judgment** (template below).
5. **Update the plan:** what hole in the system showed up, and how do you close it?
6. **Start fresh tomorrow morning**, with intention.

### Relapse log template
```
Date and time:
Where I was / which device:
HALT - hungry / angry / lonely / tired:
What happened in the hours before:
The moment when I could still have stopped:
What blocked / didn't block:
What I'm changing in the plan from today:
A kind sentence to myself:
```

> "Do not trust yourself until the day you die." (Pirkei Avot 2:4). Even after a long streak, keep the system in place. A good streak is not a reason to lower your defenses.

## 10. FAQ

**I fell after a long streak. Is all my progress erased?**
No. The clean days built muscles in you: habits, awareness and tools. One fall doesn't erase them. What matters is what you do in the next 24 hours.

**I have nocturnal emissions (wet dreams). Is that a fall?**
No. It's a natural process that isn't under your control. The plan is about what is within your choice.

**Can I do this alone?**
You can start alone, but almost everyone who succeeds over time does it with people beside them. Shame grows in the dark. Find one person you can talk to.

**How long does it take?**
There's no magic number. 90 days is a good first goal, but the real goal is to build a life in which you need this escape less.

**What if I just have no strength tonight?**
That's exactly the moment for the emergency protocol. Don't decide anything. Leave the room, eat something and go to sleep. Tomorrow you'll be stronger.
